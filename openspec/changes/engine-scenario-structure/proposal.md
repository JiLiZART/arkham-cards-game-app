## Why

Every campaign after Night of the Zealot asks the engine for scenario structure the state cannot hold: one act and one agenda (`game.s.act`, `game.s.agenda`, one `actDeck` and one `agendaDeck` pile) where Black Stars Rise runs two agenda decks, Threads of Fate three act decks and War of the Outer Gods three agendas; one card beneath a location (`flags.beneath`) where Blood on the Altar, River of Blood and the Oath stack several; `setCode` as the only way to turn a card over where VIPs become story cards, story cards become enemies and acts flip into locations; no way to remove a location (the Essex County Express, Lost in Time and Space), no out-of-play zone that keeps a card's attachments (the Hunting Horror's void), no location in the encounter deck, no "after you reveal this location" trigger, no replacement for "would be defeated" (Dim Carcosa, Possession, The Thing That Follows), no mid-scenario interlude or checkpoint (Fortune and Folly, Ice and Death), no vehicle, no named resource token, no spawn by trait or "nearest", and no "−3 instead if …" token line. The inspection reports (`dwl.md` §2, `ptc.md` §2, `tfa.md` §2.6, `eoe.md` §2, `tic.md` §2, `sides.md` M2–M4, M7, M9, M12, `boxes.md` #6–#7, #12, #15) list these as the generic gaps every campaign change depends on; `campaign-the-dunwich-legacy`, `campaign-the-path-to-carcosa`, `campaign-the-forgotten-age`, the Return-to changes, `player-cards-ptc` and `player-cards-tfa` are written against this change and name what they expect of it.

## What Changes

- **Several act and agenda decks.** `ScenarioDef.acts` and `agendas` accept one deck (a list, as today) or named decks; each deck is a pile with a current card; doom is checked against every agenda; a scenario with several decks asks the lead where doom goes when a card says "on an agenda". An entry may be a version choice (`oneOf` with a log condition, else at random), which also covers "reset the act deck to 1a".
- **Faces.** A card instance has a face; `flip` turns it to the other printed side (`<code>b`), keeping its tokens, attachments, position and controller, and moving it to the zone the new face's type lives in (a story card that becomes an enemy, an act that becomes a location). Story cards are a type with a resolution step: show the text, run its abilities, then do what the card says (flip back, remove, stay). The loader never builds a `b` face into a deck.
- **Cards beneath cards.** `beneath` becomes an ordered facedown pile per instance (`beneath:<InstId>`), for locations, enemies, acts, agendas, investigators and the scenario reference card (now an instance in a `Scenario` zone); ops put, draw, reveal and shuffle them; conditions and token X sources count them. `underAct` is folded in.
- **Location removal and the void.** `removeLocation` resolves what the rules say (investigators there are defeated unless the card moves them, enemies and attachments discarded, Victory X to the display when no clues) and fires a `locationLeavesPlay` hook; a `Void` zone keeps a card's attachments and tokens out of play, and the search ops look there when asked.
- **Locations in the encounter deck.** A single-sided location drawn or discarded to from the encounter deck is put into play by its revelation, connected as its text says; `discardEncounterUntil` accepts `type: "location"`.
- **Random setup ops** in `ScenarioDef.setup`: choose at random from a list (into play, set aside, as the act or agenda, with the rest removed or set aside), remove N at random from a pile or side deck, shuffle set-aside cards, put N random set-aside cards into play; a side deck may hold locations (the Exhibit deck).
- **Reveal trigger.** `revealLocation` opens an `afterReveal` window; a location's `trigger: "revealed"` abilities run there.
- **"Would be defeated".** `CheckDefeat` and `DefeatEnemy` open a `wouldBeDefeated` window first; a replacement ability or a scenario hook cancels the defeat and does something else.
- **Mid-scenario interludes and checkpoints.** A `ReadInterlude` step pauses the game on a story page with choices recorded into the scenario's notes; a resolution may be a `checkpoint` that records the table (locations, clues, counters, story assets, per-investigator counts) into the campaign log for the next part's setup to rebuild.
- **Vehicles.** An asset with the `vehicle` keyword at a location carries the investigators in it; enter and leave are fast actions, once each per round; a scenario can forbid entering.
- **Named tokens.** `tokens:<name>` counters on any card or investigator, with ops, an amount and a condition.
- **Spawn and prey vocabulary.** `spawn` becomes a target expression (a location name, a trait, empty, nearest or farthest to a point, connected to the drawer, unrevealed if able, a choice among several); `prey` gains `nearestTo`. The same expression parser serves Patrol (`engine-generic-keywords`).
- **Conditional token modifiers.** `TokenEffect.instead: { when: Condition; modifier }`.
- **Scenario facts.** `ScenarioFacts` gains `resigned` and `defeated` lists so the flow's "resigned or defeated" question (`campaign-flow-compiler-fixes`) is answered by the engine.
- **BREAKING**: `ENGINE_VERSION` 8; saved games from 7 are not loaded. `flags.beneath` and `flags.underAct` are removed; `currentAct(game)` and `currentAgenda(game)` take an optional deck name.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

None.

### Modified Capabilities

- `game-engine`: act and agenda decks (several, versions), locations (removal, the void, revealed window, in the encounter deck), enemies (spawn expressions, prey nearest to, would-be-defeated), chaos bag (conditional modifiers, X over beneath piles), encounter deck (locations), damage and defeat (replacement), and new requirements for faces and story cards, cards beneath cards, vehicles, named tokens, interludes and checkpoints.
- `campaign-play`: data-defined scenario setup (random ops, side decks of locations, versions), resolutions (checkpoint, resigned and defeated facts).
- `card-content`: card record format (`spawn` expression, `vehicle` keyword, story type, a `b` face is never a deck card).
